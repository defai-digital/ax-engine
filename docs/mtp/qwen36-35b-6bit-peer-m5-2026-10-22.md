# Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B AXQ 6-bit MTP: AX Engine, MTPLX, OMLX and mlx-lm on an M5 Max

Apple M5 Max 128 GB, 2026-10-22. AX Engine 7.8.0, MTPLX 2.12.2, OMLX 0.7.2, mlx-lm 0.32.2
(each the latest release that day). Pack `AutomatosX/AX-Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MLX-AXQ-6bit-MTP`
@ `6a4c2207`. Contract, per-lane numbers, provenance and the reducers:
[campaign record](../../benchmarks/results/mtp-axq-peer/2026-10-22-apple-m5-max-128gb-qwen36-35b-6bit/).
This run replaces the Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B rows of the
[2026-09-21 campaign](qwen-gemma-peer-m5-2026-09-21.md) (MTPLX 127.87 tok/s, OMLX
unsupported), which the numbers below overturn in two places.

Time to first token: AX 137 and 204 ms, MTPLX 185 and 229, OMLX 301 and 366, mlx-lm 298 and
358. End-to-end tok/s (prefill included), default configurations: AX 219.2 and 203.8, MTPLX
177.3 and 201.0, OMLX 168.5 and 188.9, mlx-lm 100.9 and 98.0.

- AX decode is 1.24x MTPLX on `flappy` and 0.98x on `long_code` (0.95x against MTPLX's best
  depth), 1.08x over all eight prompts; 2.0x AX direct; about 2.2x mlx-lm. AX prefill is 1.13x
  to 1.34x MTPLX and about 2x OMLX and mlx-lm.
- Every MTP runtime was swept over draft depth (MTPLX and OMLX 1 to 3, AX default controller
  and fixed 1 and 2). Deeper is faster on `flappy`; MTPLX peaks at depth 2 on `long_code`.
- A reverse-order pass agrees with the forward pass to within 0.3% on every default lane.

## Two corrections to the previous campaign

**OMLX was undersold by 2.4x.** AXQ packs keep the upstream MTP head byte-for-byte in
`mtp.safetensors` with raw HF-delta RMSNorm gains (`mtp_norm_layout: raw_hf_delta`) while the
trunk is stored as MLX multipliers. AX Engine reads the declaration and MTPLX detects a delta
head; OMLX repairs only four of the seven head norms, so the head drafts almost nothing: 3.8%
draft acceptance on this pack (about 4% on the Swift-1.5 27B pack on 2026-10-13) and MTP
slower than no MTP, with no error. On a head with all seven norms shifted OMLX reaches 97.6%
acceptance and the rates above. AXQuant's `export-runtime --target omlx` now builds that
variant for the Qwen 3.5, 3.6 and 3.8 families (profile `qwen35-mtp-v1`), and
`check-runtime-compatibility` reports the raw-delta head as `requires-export` for OMLX. The
trunk shards stay byte-identical.

**MTPLX's long-code lead is prompt-copy drafting, not its MTP head.** MTPLX proposes the
prompt's continuation as one verified block whenever the generated tail matches a prompt
n-gram, skipping the MTP head for that cycle (on by default; `MTPLX_CONTEXT_COPY=0` turns it
off). On `long_code_test_stubs` (90.5% of the output's words sit in a prompt 4-gram) it needs
12 MTP-head cycles for 256 tokens against 64 for pure MTP and runs at 347 tok/s; with copy off
that prompt runs at 208 and the suite at 211. Copy is a net loss on `flappy` (203.2 on, 214.9
off). AX Engine's n-gram path drafts zero tokens on this hybrid linear-attention MoE target.
Pure MTP head against MTPLX with copy off: AX 1.17x on `flappy`, 1.15x on `long_code`. The
2% deficit on `long_code` is the price of having no prompt-copy drafter.

## Limits

Single host. TTFT scopes differ (AX through HTTP, MTPLX from `prompt_eval_time_s`, OMLX and
mlx-lm in process). MTPLX needs `--allow-unverified-model` (a verification badge, not a
compatibility gap) and uses its shipped MLX 0.32.2. All runtimes ran text only. Text
agreement with AX direct decode is 5 of 8 prompts for AX, mlx-lm and MTPLX and 6 of 8 for
OMLX, so the differences are near-tie flips between kernels. AX was built from a branch
commit that was unmerged when recorded.
